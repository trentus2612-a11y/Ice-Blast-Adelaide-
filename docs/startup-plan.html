How we start without dropping thirty grand

Sell the jobs first. Hire the gear for the day. Let the customers' deposits pay for it. Buy nothing until the work is real.

The problem with the obvious plan

The machine that does everything on our website, properly, with Australian parts and service, is the Kärcher IB 7/40. Australian retail is $31,102 ex GST for the Classic and $40,822 for the Advanced.

Then the compressor. Kärcher wants a minimum of 8 bar at 140 CFM, which is a 30kW-plus three phase screw compressor, another $15,000 to $25,000, plus getting three phase into the shed.

Call it $50,000 to $60,000 before we've bought a single bin of dry ice, to service a market none of us have tested yet. We're not doing that.

The cheap eBay units around $3,000 to $6,000 are real, but they're carbon-cleaning machines. Roughly half the airflow of a commercial unit, a 7.5kg hopper when an engine takes about 15kg, a 3m hose, and no Australian service agent. Fine for engine bays, wrong for the $2,200 underbody job.

The seven steps

  1. Register and insure

    ABN, business name, public liability. Under $1,600 between the three of us and we can legally trade.

  2. Get hire quoted as a package

    Ring Cold Jet Australia, who have the biggest rental fleet in the country and run a try-before-you-buy program with training included, and Cryonomic, who do rent-to-buy where part of the rental goes toward the purchase. Ask for a day and week rate covering the blaster, the compressor and the ice delivered. The headline machine rate never includes the other two. That number is our cost of goods.

  3. Sell before we have anything

    Twenty phone calls. Local mechanics, transmission shops, restorers, detailers. Posts in the 4WD and car club groups. We're not pretending to own a machine, we're booking work for a specific date. Nobody asks whether the gear is ours.

  4. Take deposits

    20% on anything over $1,500, which is already on the website. Three jobs booked with deposits means the hire is paid for before we pick the machine up. This is the mechanism that makes the whole thing cost us nothing.

  5. Hire only when the work is booked

    Never on spec. Three or more jobs locked to a date, stacked into one day or one weekend so we pay for one hire period and run it flat out. Take the operator training on day one if it's offered.

  6. Shoot everything

    Same spot, same light, before and after, every job. Those photos fill the gallery and sell the next twenty bookings. Ask every happy customer for a Google review before we leave. Photos and reviews are the two things we can't buy later.

  7. Buy when the hire bill annoys us

    Keep running hire days until the rental cost clearly exceeds a monthly rent-to-buy payment. That's the buy signal, and it's a number not a feeling. If we never hit it, we found out for a few hundred dollars instead of thirty grand.

Two things we need to be straight about

Don't sign a workshop lease yet

A unit in Golden Grove is a monthly commitment before we've proven anything, and it's the single biggest risk in this plan. For hire days we can work out of a shed, a mate's workshop, or on the customer's own site. The lease comes after the bookings justify it, not before.

Three of us is a lot for two jobs a weekend

At the pace we'll start at there isn't enough money to pay three people. That's fine if we all keep our day jobs and treat this as a side thing while it grows, but it needs saying out loud now rather than after someone quits expecting an income.

Agree upfront: nobody draws money until it's consistently there. Everything goes back into hire days, insurance and eventually the machine.

What we do this week

  • Agree the split, and agree nobody draws money early
  • Register the ABN and business name
  • Get public liability quotes
  • Ring Cold Jet and Cryonomic for package hire rates and rent-to-buy terms
  • Find a dry ice supplier who delivers to the northeast, get per-kg plus freight and bin hire separately
  • Make the twenty phone calls. This is the one that tells us whether this is a business
  • Put the socials up and the site live

The twenty calls cost nothing and take an afternoon. If fifteen of them say they'd use us regularly, we've got something. If most say no thanks, we've saved ourselves a fortune finding out.
