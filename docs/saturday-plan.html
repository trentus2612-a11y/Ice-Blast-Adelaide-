The Saturday plan

Book the jobs first. Take deposits. Hire the machine for the day. Own nothing until the work is real.

One loop. It funds itself from the first run.

How it works

  1. Sell before we have anything

    We ring around and book jobs for a date a few weeks out. Say a Saturday in October. We're not saying we've got a machine and we're not saying we haven't, it just never comes up.

    What we say is: "We can do your engine bay on Saturday the 18th, $300."

    Customers never ask who owns the gear. Plumbers hire trailers, sparkies hire scissor lifts. Nobody blinks.

  2. Take the deposits

    When someone books, 20% up front on the bigger jobs. A $2,200 underbody books in with $440 paid. Two of those and there's $880 in the account before Saturday morning.

    That money does two jobs. It pays the hire bill so none of us is out of pocket, and it stops people cancelling the night before, which they absolutely will if cancelling is free.

  3. Hire the machine

    Once three or more jobs are locked to that Saturday, we book the gear for the day. Not before.

    If only one job books in, we push the date and keep selling.

    The work comes first, the machine comes second. Never the other way around.

  4. Do the Saturday

    Pick it up in the morning, run all three jobs back to back, collect the balance off each customer, drop the machine back that afternoon. One hire fee, three jobs.

    That's why we stack them. A hire day costs the same whether we do one job or four, so every extra job squeezed into it is nearly pure margin.

  5. Then do it again

    The money from that Saturday funds the next one. The photos from it sell the next round of bookings.

    Every fortnight, or every week, depending on how the calls go.

Why this is the smart version

We never own anything

If the phone stops ringing, we just stop hiring. No machine sitting in a shed depreciating, no workshop lease, no loan with our names on it.

Nobody quits their job

It's Saturdays. None of us has to bet our income on this working. If it grows into something, we can talk about that then.

The one thing that decides it

It isn't the machine and it isn't the pricing. It's whether we can fill a Saturday from twenty phone calls.

That's the only real test, and we can run it this week for nothing.
