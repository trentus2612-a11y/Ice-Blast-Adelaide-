# ICE BLAST ADELAIDE — Equipment List

Everything needed to run jobs out of the Golden Grove workshop, plus what's needed to go mobile. Ordered by whether you can start without it.

---

## 1. Can't start without it

### Dry ice blasting machine
The unit itself. Comes as a package with blast hose, air hose, applicator gun and nozzle, so you don't buy those separately.

- Runs off a standard power outlet, drawing roughly 6 amps, so no special electrical work needed
- Ask the supplier for the exact CFM the model needs before you buy anything else. Everything below depends on that number
- Get at least two nozzle options if offered. Nozzle choice changes the job more than machine brand does

### Air compressor
This is where people get it wrong. **Don't pick by horsepower.** A compressor with a big motor can still fail to deliver sustained airflow at pressure, and an undersized one causes pressure drop mid-blast, which shows up as uneven, patchy cleaning.

- Pellet machines typically need around 100 CFM at 80 psi. Some units are specced at a minimum of 85 CFM at 100 psi, and the common working range is 100 to 140 CFM
- Match the machine's stated requirement, then add headroom. Don't buy right on the line
- A standard workshop compressor is almost certainly not enough. Fixed shop units usually top out well below the volume a full blasting system needs
- Options: tow-behind diesel (mobile, loud, thirsty) or a big electric screw compressor bolted down in the workshop (quieter, cheaper to run, not mobile)
- New mobile diesel units run roughly $12,000 to $25,000. Used, roughly $3,500 to $11,000 depending on condition

### Aftercooler and air treatment
Not optional if you're on a diesel compressor. Diesel units put out hot, humid air, and hot air makes the pellets sublimate on the way down the hose. They arrive smaller than intended and the machine cleans worse than it should.

- Aftercooler: air-cooled radiator with a 12V fan plus a moisture trap
- Moisture separator and filtration depending on the compressor and how humid the day is
- Symptom to watch for: machine seems to be working but results are weak and inconsistent. Usually wet or hot air, not the machine

### Insulated dry ice storage bin
- One to start, a second once you're running back-to-back jobs
- Dry ice sublimates constantly, so buy per job at first rather than stockpiling and watching your money evaporate
- Bin hire is often available from the gas supplier, worth asking about instead of buying

### Dedicated ice scoop
Cheap, and non-negotiable. Pellets sit at around −79°C and direct contact causes immediate tissue damage, even through thin gloves. Never hand-load the hopper.

## 2. PPE, per person

You've got three partners, so three sets. Don't share a set between operators.

- **Cryogenic gloves** — rated for handling dry ice, not general work gloves
- **Safety glasses plus a face shield** — the glasses aren't enough on their own, you're firing particles at high velocity
- **Hearing protection, doubled up** — earplugs *and* over-ear muffs. These machines are genuinely loud and eight hours of it will cost you your hearing
- **FFP3 mask** — whatever's on the surface (oil, grease, biological muck) gets airborne when you blast it
- **Closed-toe safety boots**
- **Overalls** you don't care about

### CO2 monitor
CO2 is around 1.5 times denser than air, so it sinks and pools at low level rather than dispersing upwards. That matters for you specifically because you'll be doing underbody work, down low, where it collects.

- Essential for any enclosed or poorly ventilated space
- Exposure guidance: averaged over a working day, stay under 5,000 ppm, with a short-term ceiling around 30,000 ppm for no more than 15 minutes
- At 3 to 5% concentration you get headaches and rapid breathing. At 8 to 10% it's nausea, cramps and potential unconsciousness
- Worth a call to SafeWork SA about confined space requirements before you take on any tank, container or pit work

---

## 3. Workshop setup

- **Ventilation** — airflow at floor level, not just a roller door. Low-level extraction is the one that actually matters given CO2 sinks
- **Vehicle hoist or ramps** — you can't do underbody work lying on a creeper with a blast gun. This is probably your biggest workshop expense after the compressor
- **Good lighting, including underbody work lights** — you can't clean what you can't see, and it doubles as your photo lighting
- **Drop sheets and masking materials** — no media to clean up, but the grime you remove goes somewhere
- **Degreaser, rags, air blow gun** — for prep and touch-ups
- **Wheel chocks and jack stands**
- **Fire extinguisher and first aid kit**
- **Bunded area or drip trays** if oily residue is coming off vehicles

---

## 4. Going mobile

- **Ute, van or trailer** — sized around the compressor, which is the bulky item
- **Tie-downs and a way to load and unload the compressor** safely, solo
- **Portable generator** if you're working somewhere without power for the blaster
- **Extension leads, heavy duty**
- **Portable work lights**
- **Insulated bin that travels well**

---

## 5. Photos and admin

- **A decent phone on a small tripod** — your before/after gallery is your marketing, and consistent framing is what makes the slider work. A $30 tripod is the highest-ROI purchase on this list
- **Quoting and invoicing** — Square, Stripe or similar for card payments on the spot
- **Public liability insurance** — before the first paid job

---

## 6. Ongoing supply

- **Dry ice pellets**, standard size is 3 × 6 mm. If you can only get blocks, they need crushing to size first, because irregular pieces bridge in the hopper and disrupt the feed
- Source from industrial gas suppliers or welding supply companies. Set up a standing delivery schedule rather than chasing it job by job
- Get per-kg pricing plus freight and bin hire quoted separately, they're often quoted as one number and it hides the real cost

---

## Questions to ask every supplier you quote

1. Exact CFM and psi this machine needs, sustained, not peak
2. Is an aftercooler included or extra
3. What nozzles come with it and what do spares cost
4. Rent-to-buy terms
5. Do you provide operator training
6. What's the service interval and where does it get serviced in SA
7. Do you have marketing imagery we're licensed to use

That last one is how you get legitimate gallery photos before your first job.
