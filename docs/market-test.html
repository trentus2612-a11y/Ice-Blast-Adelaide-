Testing the market

Before anyone buys a machine, we find out if the work is actually there. One Saturday tells us.

Advertise› Book a Saturday› Hire the machine

The three steps

  1. Advertise

    Site goes live, socials go up, and we get the word out. Posts in the 4WD and car club groups, the local Facebook pages, and phone calls to mechanics, transmission shops, restorers and detailers.

    We pick a Saturday a few weeks out and push that date. Not "we're open", but "we're doing jobs on Saturday the 18th".

  2. Book people in for that Saturday

    Every enquiry gets booked to the one date. We're stacking them deliberately, not spreading them out.

    We take a deposit on the bigger jobs so nobody cancels the night before, and so the day is paid for before it starts.

    We're not saying we own a machine and we're not saying we don't. It never comes up. Customers don't ask who owns the gear.

  3. Hire the machine for the day

    Once the Saturday is full, we book the gear. Pick it up in the morning, run the jobs back to back, drop it back that afternoon.

    One hire fee covers the whole day no matter how many jobs we do, which is exactly why we stacked them.

    If the bookings don't come, we push the date. We never hire and hope.

What the Saturday tells us

This isn't really about the money from one day. It's a test, and it has two possible results.

The day fills up

The demand is real. We keep running Saturdays, the photos and reviews build up, and we work out when it's worth owning a machine instead of hiring one.

It doesn't fill up

We found that out for the cost of some phone calls and an afternoon, instead of after signing for a machine and a workshop.

Either result is worth having. The only bad version is guessing.
